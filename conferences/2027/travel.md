## Getting to New York City

### By air

| Airport | Code | Location | Typical time to Manhattan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| John F. Kennedy International | JFK | Queens | 45–75 min |
| LaGuardia | LGA | Queens | 30–60 min |
| Newark Liberty International | EWR | New Jersey | 45–75 min |

Journey times vary considerably with traffic and time of day. Public transit is
usually the more predictable option at peak times.

### By rail

Long-distance and regional trains arrive at **Penn Station** (Amtrak, NJ Transit,
LIRR) and **Grand Central Terminal** (Metro-North), both in midtown Manhattan
and both on the subway network.

## Getting around

- **Subway and bus** — the MTA network covers the city and runs 24 hours. Tap a
  contactless card or phone at the turnstile (OMNY); a weekly fare cap applies
  automatically.
- **Taxi and ride-hailing** — widely available; fares from the airports are
  substantially higher than transit.
- **Walking** — much of Manhattan is walkable, and short trips are often faster
  on foot than by car.

## Practical notes

- **Currency** is the US dollar. Card payment is accepted almost everywhere.
- **Tipping** is customary in restaurants and for taxis.
- **Power** is 120 V, 60 Hz, with Type A and B sockets.
- **Weather** in late spring is mild but variable; bring a layer and a
  rain jacket.

## Visas

Many visitors need a visa or an ESTA authorization to enter the United States.
See [Visa Information](/2027/visa/) — start early, as processing times can be
long.
