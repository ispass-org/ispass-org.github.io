Keynote 1: The Critical Role of Simulation Tools in Today’s Computer Architecture Research

Speaker: David Kaeli (Northeastern University)

Abstract:

With each new generation of CPU and GPU, we have seen significant increases in the design complexity of these devices. Our systems today have become much more heterogeneous in nature, including new types of accelerators and memory systems. The size of many of our applications have outstripped the resources of a single device or node.  These challenges pose significant challenges for our community, as we need to deliver simulation infrastructures that can enable efficient and accurate pre-silicon performance analysis.  In this talk, we will look back at some of the major developments in simulation tools over the years, reflecting on how we have continued to answer the call for new state-of-the-art simulation tools.  We will also look to the future, envisioning new classes of tools and methodologies to meet the demands of future architects.

Bio:

David Kaeli is a Distinguished Professor in the Dept. of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University. Prior to joining Northeastern in 1993, he spent 12 years at IBM, the last 7 at T.J. Watson Research Center, Yorktown Heights, NY. His research interests include computer architecture, high performance hardware/software, performance evaluation and hardware security.  He received his PhD in Electrical Engineering from Rutgers University.  He presently serves as Editor-in-Chief of ACM TACO. He is a fellow of both the IEEE and ACM.
