Keynote 1: The JUMP 2.0 ACE Center for Evolvable Computing: Energy-efficient Distributed Computing for the Next Decade

Speaker: Josep Torrellas (UIUC)

Abstract:

The mission of the SRC/DARPA JUMP 2.0 ACE Center for Evolvable Computing is to develop new technologies to improve the energy efficiency and performance of distributed computing for the next decade. To attain high energy efficiency, the computing backbone must rely on heterogeneous ensembles of hardware accelerators that are easy to generate, deploy, and reconfigure. General-purpose cores should be specialized for different workload classes to increase their energy efficiency. Ubiquitous intelligence should be distributed across memory and storage blocks, as well as in network switches and SmartNICs. Flexible network topologies should provide the basis for accelerator-heavy datacenters that run specialized communication stacks and runtime systems that minimize data movement. Finally, since accelerators will process sensitive data, they need to be designed with security mechanisms currently used in CPUs.

Bio:

Josep Torrellas is the Saburo Muroga Professor of Computer Science at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC). He is the Director of the ACE Center. His research interests are multiprocessor computer architectures and parallel computing. Some of his contributions include thread-level speculation (TLS) architectures, the Bulk Multiprocessor concept, deterministic record and replay mechanisms, process variation mitigation techniques, and hardware defenses against speculative execution attacks. He has contributed to several experimental multiprocessor designs such as IBM’s PERCS Multiprocessor, Intel’s Runnemede Extreme-Scale Multiprocessor, Illinois Cedar, and Stanford DASH. Torrellas has received the IEEE CS Harry H. Goode Memorial Award, the UIUC Daniel C. Drucker Eminent Faculty Award, the UIUC Campus Award for Excellence in Graduate Student Mentoring, and the IEEE CS Technical Achievement Award, among other awards. He is a Fellow of IEEE, ACM, and AAAS. He has served in the Board of Directors of CRA, has been a Council Member of CRA’s CCC, and has been a member of the U.S. National Academies Board on Army Research and Development. He received a PhD from Stanford University.
