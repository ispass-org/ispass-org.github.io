Keynote 1: The Challenges of Reconciling Low Latency and High Efficiency

Prof. Christos Kozyrakis
Stanford University and Google

Low tail latency is essential for multi-tier, online applications, such as web-search, social networking, and software-as-a-service. Unfortunately, current systems introduce tradeoffs between low latency and a number of features essential for cost effective operation of public and private clouds: high utilization of servers, continuous power management, use of commodity hardware and software, etc. This talk will first analyze the main challenges of achieving low tail latency. Next, we will review recently-developed software and hardware techniques that can reduce tail latencies from milliseconds to microseconds. Finally, we will discuss open questions throughout the system stack, focusing in particular on the need for new analysis tools and interactions with emerging hardware designs.

Bio: Christos Kozyrakis is a Professor of Electrical Engineering & Computer Science at Stanford University and a Principal Engineer at Google Cloud. His current research focuses on resource efficient cloud computing, energy efficient compute and memory systems for emerging workloads, and scalable operating systems. Christos received a BS degree from the University of Crete in Greece and a PhD degree from UC Berkeley. He is a Fellow of the ACM and the IEEE, and has received several awards including the ACM SIGARCH Maurice Wilkes awards for his contributions to transactional memory technology.
