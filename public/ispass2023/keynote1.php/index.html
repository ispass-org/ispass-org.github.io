Keynote 1: Computer Architecture in the Era of Cloud Computing

Speaker: George Chrysos (Microsoft)

Abstract:

Cloud computing continues to gain prominence as the world’s computing and storage needs are served most efficiently and reliably by the economies of scale of multi-megawatt data centers deployed globally. As a leading cloud service provider, Microsoft has the both the responsibility and opportunity to shape the future generations of massive hardware deployments. The benefits and costs of the data center architecture, both in aggregate and component by component, are magnified by the enormity of hundreds of massive data centers. Good optimizations can result in savings of billions of dollars or tons of carbon. Consequently, computer architects play a pivotal role in ensuring high performance, power efficiency, sustainability, and lowering the total cost of ownership (TCO) of the fleet.

To make informed decisions, computer architects must utilize and develop tools that provide visibility into the consequences of their choices. These methods include profiling workload activity and performance, constructing representative benchmarks, building performance simulators for critical components, and modeling power consumption and TCO. It is essential to consider the overall data center optimization when making component, platform, or rack-level decisions.

This keynote presentation will explore the challenges of architecting next-generation data center components, showcase the approaches employed by the Azure Hardware Architecture Development Group at Microsoft, and present case studies in CPU SoC architecture, accelerators, and platform architecture. These examples will offer fresh perspectives on measuring success in computer architecture while emphasizing the importance of holistic data center optimization.

Bio:

George Chrysos is a seasoned computer architect with nearly 30 years of experience in the industry. As Microsoft Partner and Chief Architect of the Azure Hardware Architecture Development Group, he leverages his extensive background in various aspects of computer architecture, such as microprocessor and SoC development, performance modeling, power modeling, and workload analysis. George has 50 patents issued and seven publications in computer architecture. His career began in 1994 at Digital Equipment Corporation and Compaq, followed by a nearly 20-year tenure at Intel. In 2020, George joined Microsoft, and he currently leads an exceptional team consisting of computer architects, performance and power modelers, TCO analysts, and workload specialists. As Chief Architect, George continues to shape the future of hardware architecture within the Azure ecosystem, driving innovation in the cloud computing space.
